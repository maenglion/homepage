CASP

Cognitive–Adaptive Stress Paralinguistic.
Measuring non-verbal acoustic signals to quantify cognitive load and emotional variance.

The Paralinguistic Signal

Human speech carries two parallel streams of information: the semantic content (what is said) and the paralinguistic signal (how it is spoken). While modern AI heavily relies on Speech-to-Text (STT) to process the former, it systematically discards the latter. CASP focuses entirely on the discarded signal.

We hypothesize that variations in speech rate, pause duration, rhythm, jitter, and energy correlate strongly with internal cognitive states. A sudden increase in cognitive load, hesitation, or emotional stress manifests as measurable deviations from an individual's baseline acoustic profile.

Anchor-Point Engine and Baseline Construction

The CASP Engine does not rely on universal thresholds. Instead, it operates on a Bayesian-inspired Anchor-Point methodology. It first establishes a personalized baseline—the anchor—during a neutral state. Subsequent speech is continuously measured against this anchor, computing variance and drift in real time.

We are currently conducting sampling research to refine the anchor-point calibration process and validate the robustness of the extracted acoustic features across diverse environments.

Research Ethics and Data Protection

All data collection and sampling research for the CASP Engine are conducted in strict compliance with research ethics and data protection regulations.

  • Informed Consent: The purpose and scope of data collection are clearly communicated to all participants prior to explicit consent.
  • Sensitive Data Separation: Voice data, which may imply sensitive information such as stress levels, is processed separately from general personally identifiable information (PII).
  • Immediate Anonymization: Semantic content (STT) is entirely discarded. Only pure paralinguistic signals (numerical variance data) are extracted and stored, ensuring the impossibility of reverse identification.

Sister Methodology: SLDA

While CASP measures the acoustic variance in speech, its sister methodology, SLDA (Semantic Latent Dirichlet Allocation), tracks the semantic and logical drift in text. Both systems share the same underlying Bayesian reasoning framework: continuously updating the state of a network based on sequential observations, whether those observations are acoustic frames or semantic tokens.
